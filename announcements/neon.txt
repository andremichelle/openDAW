# Update: Meet Neon, a Phase Distortion Synthesizer!

Something new to play with. Neon is a synth in the spirit of the old Casio CZ series. There is no
filter in there, just one knob that bends a soft tone into saws, squares, and screaming resonant
sweeps. Put an envelope on that knob, and you get the whole 80s digital sound.

The fun part: it loads real CZ-101 patches. Grab any .syx file from the archives around the web,
load it from the device menu, and you are playing sounds someone programmed on the hardware forty
years ago. We spent a lot of time measuring real CZ hardware to get the envelopes and waveforms
right, so those old patches sound the way they should.

There is also ring modulation for metallic bells and a noise mode for drums. The
[manual](https://opendaw.studio/manuals/devices/instruments/neon) explains every knob. Now go make
some bell pads.

openDAW has a new instrument: Neon, a phase distortion synthesizer.

openDAW is a free, browser-based digital audio workstation. Neon recreates the phase distortion
synthesis of the classic Casio CZ series, an approach from 1984 that shapes timbre without any
filter. It runs entirely in the browser on openDAW's Rust audio engine, no installation, no plugins.

Why it matters for education:
• Phase distortion is a wonderful way to teach synthesis. One knob sweeps a pure tone into rich
  waveforms, so students hear the connection between waveform and timbre immediately.
• Students can load thousands of freely available original CZ-101 patches as .syx files and study
  how classic sounds were programmed.
• The instrument was calibrated against measurements of real CZ hardware, so what students hear is
  faithful to the original instrument.

Two synthesis lines, eight waveforms including resonant sweeps, three 8-stage envelopes per line,
ring and noise modulation, vibrato, and glide.

Free and open: https://opendaw.studio
Manual: https://opendaw.studio/manuals/devices/instruments/neon

Subject: Neon, a classic 80s synthesizer for your classroom

openDAW has a new instrument. Neon brings phase distortion synthesis, the sound of the classic
Casio CZ series from 1984, straight into the browser.

Why this matters for schools
• Phase distortion makes synthesis teachable. There is no filter. One control morphs a pure tone
  into saws, squares, pulses, and resonant sweeps, so students hear how waveform shapes timbre.
• The original CZ-101 was the affordable classroom synthesizer of its era. Neon revives it for
  every student at once, with no hardware to buy and nothing to install.
• Thousands of original CZ patches are freely available on the web as .syx files. Students can load
  them directly into Neon and study how the classic sounds were built.
• Neon was calibrated against measurements and recordings of real CZ hardware, so the sound is
  faithful to the original instrument.

What is inside
Two synthesis lines, eight waveforms including three resonant ones, three 8-stage envelopes per
line for pitch, brightness and level, ring and noise modulation, vibrato, glide, and full patch
import from Casio CZ .syx files.

Getting started
Add Neon from the instrument list and play. The manual explains every section:
https://opendaw.studio/manuals/devices/instruments/neon

openDAW is free and open. Happy teaching,
