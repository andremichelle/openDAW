openDAW announcement decisions and style
========================================
Notes to remember how the user wants announcements written. Keep this updated.

Writing style (all channels)
• No dashes (em or en dashes). Use commas or periods instead.
• No semicolons. Split into two sentences, or use a comma.
• Plain language. No marketing speak (for example, avoid "The point:").

Per channel
• Discord: supports markdown. Big headline uses "# " and starts with "Update: ", in Title Case
  (keep brand casing like openDAW and Nextcloud). Links use markdown masked links [label](url).
  The tone is familiar, we talk to our community like friends. No business speech, no endless
  feature lists, no spec sheets. Short flowing paragraphs that tell what is fun about the thing.
  Use "### " sublines sparingly, only when a post really needs structure. No emoji.
• LinkedIn: professional. Hashtags at the end.
• Newsletter: focus on the schools angle. No greeting line (no "Hi ..."). No beta or pilot note.

Links
• openDAW: https://opendaw.studio
• Nextcloud manual: https://opendaw.studio/manuals/nextcloud

Feature framing (Nextcloud)
• Audience: schools and music classes first.
• Each student has their own Nextcloud account and own space, isolated from other students.
• Projects and assets stay on the school's own Nextcloud. openDAW talks to it directly. Nothing
  passes through openDAW servers.
• Samples and soundfonts are deduplicated, stored once and reused.
• openDAW is free and open.
• This is not a beta. Do not call it a beta.

Feature framing (Neon)
• A phase distortion synthesizer in the spirit of the Casio CZ series (1984). Say "in the spirit of",
  do not claim to be a Casio product.
• Education angle: phase distortion is teachable synthesis, one control morphs waveform and timbre,
  no filter involved.
• Real CZ-101 .syx patches load directly, thousands are freely available.
• Calibrated against measurements and recordings of real CZ hardware. Faithful, not "emulation
  perfect", avoid absolute claims.
• Manual: https://opendaw.studio/manuals/devices/instruments/neon

Feature framing (Cubed)
• A monophonic acid bass synthesizer in the spirit of the silver box. Say "in the spirit of", do not
  name the manufacturer and do not claim to be their product.
• The sound comes from the sequencer, not the knobs. Accent placement and slides are the story.
• The accent circuit recharges like the hardware, so an accent after a rest hits harder than one in
  a dense run.
• No education angle. Cubed is an acid machine and it is fun, that is the whole pitch. This includes
  the newsletter, which drops the schools framing for this one.
• History, get it right: the box started acid house and walked into techno. It did not "shape
  electronic dance music", EDM was not even a phrase back then.
• Voice: we are openDAW, not a teacher. Talk to people who want to make a noise tonight. Never
  explain the panel or narrate what a control does.
• Cubed is a clone. Never present its limits as our design decision ("we made it small on purpose").
  The panel is short because the original was a practice bass for guitarists, sold badly, was
  discontinued after a couple of years, and ended up cheap in second-hand shops where people used it
  wrong. That is the story worth telling.
• Manual: https://opendaw.studio/manuals/devices/instruments/cubed

Feature framing (Modulators)
• Assigned by right-clicking any control: device knob, mixer fader, send, MIDI CC.
• Four kinds: LFO, Steps, Random, Macro. One source drives many parameters, each with its own depth.
• Modulation is added on top of automation, it does not replace it.
• Timed kinds keep running while the transport is stopped and re-align to the song position on
  pause, resume, locate and loop jumps.
• Random is seeded, so renders match what was heard. Say reproducible, not "deterministic".
• A depth is a parameter too, so a modulator can drive another modulator's depth.
• The pitch is what modulation DOES: organic movement that never quite repeats, or tight predictable
  movement locked to the tempo. It injects life into any kind of music, not only electronic. Never
  call it "a core idea that is hard to explain".
• Education angle: students experiment with different kinds of movement to shape their sounds. No
  UI mechanics (no "right-click"), no Macro fader, no instructions to teachers.
• Manual: https://opendaw.studio/manuals/modulation
