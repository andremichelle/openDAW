# openDAW 1.1

## New Features

### Random Modulator: Bias and Focus
Bias sets the centre the random values gather around. Focus pulls the values towards that centre, from untouched at zero to the bias itself at full focus. Both are automatable and available in the scripting API.

_Source: openDAW_

### Mixer Device Rows
Every channel strip lists its MIDI effects above the input and its audio effects below it. Toggle a device, click its name to jump to it in the device panel, drag devices to reorder them or move them to other strips, and add new ones with the add button. Both sections can be collapsed to a device count.

_Source: openDAW_

### Compact Mixer Volume
When the mixer gets too short for the volume fader, each channel strip switches to a volume knob below the panning knob with a small stereo peak meter beside it. Faders and peak meters thin out their scale lines and dB labels as they get shorter.

_Source: openDAW_

### Playfield Pads Play Any Instrument
Every Playfield pad holds its own MIDI effects, its own instrument and its own audio effects, with gain, pan, mute, solo and exclude. Sample pads play with Nano, so every Nano control is available on each pad. An empty pad offers a sample button and an instrument button, which opens the device browser with presets, WebCLAPs and soundfonts. A pad only hears its own note and its scope shows the instrument before the pad's effects and mix. A Playfield, a Composite or Tape cannot go on a pad. Scripts put an instrument on a pad with addPad and read those pads back. Old projects and presets open with every slot turned into a pad with the same sample, envelope, pitch, mix, effects and automation.

_Source: [#288](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/issues/288) by kaiartikkas, [#411](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/issues/411)_

### Playfield Pads on the Timeline
A pad dragged onto the timeline, or moved with Move to Own Track, becomes its own track with its instrument, effects, mix, automation and only the notes that trigger it. Copy, Cut and Paste on a pad work like an audio unit: a copied pad pastes onto another pad, onto the timeline as a track or into a Composite as a layer, and any track pasted onto a pad brings its notes along on the pad's key. A track dragged by its instrument onto a pad becomes that pad the same way, with Alt it stays and the pad gets a copy.

_Source: [#409](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/issues/409) by kaiartikkas_

### Cubed Pattern Menu
Right-click anywhere on Cubed for Octave Up and Down, Double, Reverse and Invert around a typed note. A pattern exports to a note region or a note clip exactly as the sequencer plays it, and a selected region or clip imports back into the pattern.

_Source: [#352](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/issues/352) by BeatMaxPrediction_

### Chord Keys
A new MIDI effect that plays the chords of a key from single keys. Each white key plays the chord on its step of the key, and held black keys change it: C# adds the 7th, D# the 9th, F# and G# play sus2 and sus4, A# the next inversion. Pressed while a chord sounds, a black key adds or removes only its own note. It follows the project key, and Voice Leading plays each chord in the inversion closest to the previous one.

_Source: openDAW_

### Sampler
A new instrument that plays many samples by key and velocity. Each sample is a zone with its key range, velocity range, root key, tuning, gain, start, end and loop, which can run while the note is held or always, forwards or alternating. Zones live in groups with round robin, choke and release trigger. The panel has a filter, an amp and a mod envelope, two LFOs and a list of modulations from a source to a target. The Editor button in the header opens the sample zone window, which shows the map over a piano with tools to map by name, spread keys and velocities, crossfade and duplicate. SFZ and SF2 files import, the instrument exports as a soundfont, and scripts can build one through the scripting API. Soundfonts of the library play in the Sampler: double-click one, drop it on the timeline or on a Sampler, or pick it in the device browser, and choose the preset (a soundfont with one preset applies it at once). The zone map lights up the zones that play, and a click on the waveform plays the sample as it is, a second click stops it.

_Source: [#538](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/issues/538)_

### Script Dialogs
A script can ask for values with openDAW.showDialog. It describes text, number, slider, checkbox and dropdown fields, the studio shows them in a dialog and hands the values back, or nothing when the dialog is cancelled.

_Source: openDAW_

### Surgeon
A new instrument that plays metallic FM themes for raw techno by itself while the transport runs. A pitch loop and four lanes grow from seeds and can mutate from pass to pass, and each lane can move any of the sixteen sound controls. Eight patterns switch on the bar, by a click or by automation, and clicking a pattern while recording writes its automation on that bar. Off silences the loops, then Surgeon plays the notes of its track with the selected pattern's sound. The Roll buttons throw new pitches, lanes or sounds. Every pattern can play through a groove of its own, restart its loops every few steps and shift where they start. A clicked pattern copies and pastes with Cmd+C and Cmd+V. Every control is automatable and scripts reach all of it through the scripting API.

_Source: openDAW_

### openDrum
Ten new drum instruments that synthesize every hit without samples: Kick, Snare, Clap, Tom, Rim, Hat, Shaker, Cymbal, Bell and Perc. They play on a track or on a Playfield pad, where the pad's key sets the note. Each editor shows the hit its values make, its level and pitch over time and a dot for every hit that sounds, and pressing the display plays it. Mono cuts the ringing hit with each new note, Poly lets hits overlap, and Gate also cuts the hit when its note ends. Pan places each device between left and right. Exclusive pads choke each other, so a closed hat cuts an open one. The Kick can add a rumbling tail that ducks under every hit, its length follows the song's tempo. Drive starts at the clean level, so a little Drive is a little louder. Every value is automatable, and scripts create and edit all ten through the scripting API. Two Playfield kits come as stock presets, openDrum 808 and openDrum 909. All ten share their own icon, a grid of dots that grows to the center.

_Source: openDAW_

### Euclid
A new MIDI effect that plays Euclidean rhythms, made to drive a Playfield. Each lane spreads its pulses evenly over its steps on its own note and rate, with rotation, accents on an inner ring and bursts that repeat a hit with a fading or rising level. Chance and gate work per lane, mute and solo per lane in each pattern, so switching patterns by automation also mutes lanes, and lanes reorder by drag. Eight patterns switch on the next bar, by a click or by automation, each with its own groove, and clicking a pattern while recording writes its automation on the bar where it switches. Roll throws new rhythms for one lane or all of them, starting from a different seed in every new device, a pattern copies and pastes, a lane's rhythm copies and pastes onto any lane of another pattern, Add Lane scrolls to the new lane, and a pattern exports to a note region. Incoming notes pass through, and scripts build lanes through the scripting API.

_Source: openDAW_

### Record Studio
Record Studio at the end of the openDAW menu, or Ctrl+Shift+E, records the studio with its sound as an MP4 video in Chrome and Edge. After sharing the tab a dialog waits until the studio is arranged, then the video is written straight to a file on disk. The red button at the right of the header or the same shortcut ends the recording.

_Source: openDAW_

### Guitar
A new instrument, a six string guitar built from physical string models, that strums the chords of the chord track with a rhythm pattern or plays the notes of its track on its strings. Sixteen pattern slots hold strokes like down, up, palm mute, chuck, chop, bass and picks, filled from a library of patterns in many styles and switched by automation. Sixteen custom shapes are built by clicking finger positions on the neck, and automating the chord source plays an own progression with them, independent of the chord track and heard by no other device. Each string of a step can bend up by a quarter, half, one or two semitones and fall back within the step, and the chuck is a short thunk that still carries the chord. Patterns run on from the start of the song and play every step in any signature. Strings can be retuned on the neck, a Bass tuning plays four strings, and a string can be switched off. Clicking a string's note under the neck plays that string, slots copy and paste with Cmd+C and Cmd+V or the right-click menu, and a Clear button empties the pattern. Position, tuning, pick, spot, damping, tension and ten bodies from dreadnought to electric bass shape the sound, Tilt balances bass and treble strings in a strum. Scripts reach all of it through the scripting API.

_Source: openDAW_

### Connect an AI Agent
An AI agent like Claude Code takes over tedious and repetitive edits on your project, like renaming and recolouring tracks, routing them into buses, putting the same effect on many tracks or editing selected notes in bulk. Connect Agent in the openDAW menu offers the openDAW server or a relay on your own computer, and Copy Command copies the one line to run in the terminal. The agent reads the scripting API, runs scripts in the open project and sees what you work on: the device you edit, the open region and the selection. From its first call a dialog shows what it does, the elapsed time and the number of calls, everything it changed becomes one undo step, and Cancel stops it. While an agent is connected the footer shows its status next to Online, a click on it or Disconnect Agent in the menu ends the connection. Running the copied command again replaces an older setup. The manual page Connect AI Agent explains the setup, the dialog links to it. Asked to compose, the agent does what was asked and adds no musical suggestions of its own, the manual page has a section on making music with an agent.

_Source: [#553](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/issues/553) by CVFA_

### FX Composite Layer Send
Each layer of the FX Composite has a play button next to mute and solo, in the layer list and inside the layer. It gates what goes into the layer while its output stays on, so delay and reverb tails ring out. A click toggles it, holding it sends only while held. It is automatable and MIDI learnable, follows its automation while playing, and existing projects play as before. Scripts reach it as entry.send.

_Source: openDAW_

### Time Selection
A new Time Selection tool next to the Pointer in the timeline header (T and V switch between them). Drag over any tracks, empty or not, to select a stretch of time. Hold Shift, or drag in the marker, tempo, signature, key and chord tracks, to select it across the whole song. Move and resize the selection, then right-click it for Delete Time, which removes it and pulls everything after it earlier, or Insert Time, which pushes everything from its start later. Regions crossing an edge are cut there, and folded group buses take their tracks along. Delete removes the selected time, Cmd+I inserts it, and tempo curves keep their shape at the cut. When a cut leaves notes or automation points in a region that can no longer be heard, openDAW offers to clean them up. Insert Region (Alt+I) inserts time and fills it with new regions on the note and automation tracks. Bounce renders the selected instruments into one sample on a new Tape track, with an optional pre-roll in quarter notes so held notes and echoes reach into it.

_Source: [#460](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/issues/460)_

### Custom Server Backup
Keep the backup on your own webspace, next to Dropbox and Google Drive. openDAW creates a small PHP file with a personal token, you upload it to any hosting package with PHP and HTTPS and enter its address. The backup goes from the browser straight to that server, large files travel in chunks, several at once, and the same server keeps several browsers and computers in sync. Custom Server is the first entry under Backup & Sync and in the Backup menu.

_Source: openDAW_

### Reverse Audio
Right-click an audio region or a launcher clip and choose Reverse, or press Shift+V, to play it backwards. Only the part of the sample the region or clip plays is reversed, so other regions using the same sample stay as they are. Warp markers are mirrored, so every part keeps its stretch. Undo brings back the original.

_Source: pull request [#562](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/pull/562) by polinom_

### Community Presets
The stock presets live in the public [opendaw-commons](https://github.com/andremichelle/opendaw-commons) repository, next to the community scripts and WebCLAPs, so anyone can add a preset. Save for Community… on one of your presets in the device browser saves its file for a pull request, and the manual page Community Presets walks through it. The device browser shows the author and license of every stock preset, and the size of the samples and soundfonts a stock preset loads. Scripts save an instrument as a preset file through the scripting API. The library adds multi-sample Sampler presets from the Versilian Community Sample Library: mallets, harps, pianos, organs, an ocarina, drum kits and percussion. A list of tags next to the device browser narrows the entries to one kind, such as Drums, Mallets or Reverb, with the number of entries per tag, and presets show only their own tags. Filter, Scale Matrix, Chord Keys and Spielwerk are listed under Utility. The device browser uses more of a wide screen, and the search lines up with the list below it.

_Source: [#255](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/issues/255)_

### Automation Steps
Every automation track has a Steps setting in the value editor. 0 is continuous, 2 is on and off, any other number gives that many equal steps. Creating, moving, painting and typing values snap to the steps, and the value axis shows the step lines. Painting drops points that do not change the automation, and Alt with Cmd or Ctrl paints steps that hold instead of ramping. Scripts read and set the steps of an automation track.

_Source: [#38](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/issues/38)_

### Mid/Side Split
A new audio effect that sends the mid and the side of a signal through their own effect chains and sums them back, for mid/side EQ, a mono low end or widening. Revamp in a branch shows only that branch's spectrum, and solo on Mid or Side prelistens it. An untouched split passes the signal through, and a latent effect in one branch stays aligned with the other. Available in the device browser, presets and scripting, with a manual page. Spectrum displays show the Side layer and any other anti-phase content, mono content reads as before.

_Source: [#90](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/issues/90)_

### All Layers
A new button in the header of every composite (FX Composite, Stereo Split, Mid/Side Split, Frequency Split, Instrument Composite and Playfield) shows all its layers below each other, each with its full device chain, gain, pan, mute, solo and level meter. Compare layers at one glance, solo one while listening, and open a script editor from any layer, closing it returns to All Layers. A composite inside a layer has its own button. Press the button again or the close button to return.

_Source: [#568](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/issues/568)_

### Stage Website
Your own music website for the tracks you make in openDAW, on any webspace with PHP. The Stage button in the project info publishes the mixdown, cover and project info, and optionally the project itself so listeners can open it in openDAW. Publishing again replaces the track and keeps its likes and comments. Visitors listen with a waveform player and, with Google login, like, comment and follow, followers get a mail for every new track. You moderate everything on a private admin page, which also updates the website with one click. The token is kept by your password manager, never in the browser storage. The website files and their manual are at [github.com/andremichelle/opendaw-stage](https://github.com/andremichelle/opendaw-stage). A published cover is scaled down to at most 1000 by 1000 pixels, the project keeps its own cover unchanged.

_Source: openDAW_

### Create a WebCLAP with AI
Anyone can now build their own audio effects, instruments and MIDI effects for openDAW by describing them to an AI coding agent, no programming needed. One line, `npx skills add https://webclap.opendaw.studio -g`, teaches the agent how openDAW plugins are built. The agent then builds the plugin, checks it and opens a preview in the browser that runs it in openDAW's engine, with the device panel and the plugin window, and reloads it after every change. The finished plugin looks and behaves like a stock device in the device panel, opens a larger window and has every control automatable. Load Plugin, Import WebCLAP... brings it into a project, and on request the agent shares it with every openDAW user through a pull request to opendaw-commons. The manual page Create a WebCLAP with AI walks through it.

_Source: openDAW_

### MIDI Mapping
The MIDI button in the header turns MIDI on, and once it is on it opens the Mapping screen. While the screen is open, every parameter that can be mapped shows a purple ring and mapped ones an orange ring. Click a parameter, move a knob or fader on your controller and the mapping is made. The list shows every mapping with its MIDI device and channel, CC, track, device and parameter. Each mapping has its own range with Min and Max in the parameter's unit, Min above Max inverts the control, and Takeover chooses whether the parameter jumps, waits until the controller picks it up or scales towards it. Endless encoders work with three relative encodings. A mapping listens to all devices and channels or to one of them, the list can be searched and sorted, and mappings export to and import from a JSON file. Existing mappings work as before.

_Source: [#571](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/pull/571), [#572](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/pull/572) by BLyndon_

## Changes

### Balanced Mastering
Mastering matches a reference more gently. The matching curves keep their shape and level off smoothly toward 9 dB instead of running flat at 12 dB, so no band is boosted or cut too far. The level rises only until the loudest peaks pass the limiter by about 2 dB, so the limiter shapes transients instead of squashing the mix, and Gain still makes it louder on purpose. The built-in profiles aim at a moderate loudness, and Bright is a gentler variation of Balanced. Analyse again to apply this to a mastered project.

_Source: openDAW_

### Samples Take Half the Space
A sample is stored as 16 bit when nothing is lost by it, which halves its size in the browser storage and in project bundles. The studio also keeps a sample in memory only while something shows or edits it. Samples stored earlier stay as they are until converted on the new Sample Optimisation page, reached from the storage page. It lists your own samples, tells which convert without loss, which by rounding and which stay 32 bit, and converts the selected ones.

_Source: openDAW_

### Audio Fade Handles
Hovering an audio clip shows larger fade handles in the clip colour. Drag a square to set the fade length, and the circle in the middle of the fade up or down to shape its curve. New fades start straight. Splitting a clip keeps the fade-in on the left piece and the fade-out on the right piece, with no fades at the cut. Holding Shift while dragging a handle moves it at a fifth of the speed and snaps near 25, 50 and 75 percent. Resting on a handle shows its value, which stays while dragging. The handles fade in and out when the pointer enters or leaves a clip.

_Source: pull requests [#547](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/pull/547), [#546](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/pull/546) and [#549](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/pull/549) by polinom_

### Clip Colours in the Context Menu
The context menu of clips and regions starts with the six clip colours as small squares. A click colours every selected clip in one undo step, and the current colour is outlined when all selected clips share it. Pressing on a clip, dragging onto a square and releasing works too. The Color submenu is gone.

_Source: pull request [#544](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/pull/544) by polinom_

### Pinch to Zoom the Timeline
A trackpad pinch over the arrangement or an editor zooms the time axis around the pointer, like Shift and the mouse wheel. Ctrl and the mouse wheel zoom the same way. A scroll gesture keeps to the direction it starts in, so scrolling the tracks no longer moves the time axis and scrolling in time no longer moves the tracks.

_Source: pull request [#540](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/pull/540) by polinom_

### New Instruments Start Without a Region
A new instrument no longer creates a note region. An empty note lane shows the hint "Double-click to create a note-region" while Visible Help & Hints is on.

_Source: openDAW_

### Group Buses Fed by Sinks
In the mixer, a group bus without member tracks shows no collapse button and no group rail.

_Source: openDAW_

### Storage Assets by Kind
The Assets tab of the storage page lists samples, soundfonts and WebCLAPs as groups, each sorted by name. Files that cannot be read are listed below the summary with the project or asset they belong to.

_Source: openDAW_

### Script Results and Lookup by Id
A script hands back the value it returns, and project.findByUuid finds any unit, device, track, region or note by its id.

_Source: [#553](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/issues/553)_

### Paste Shows a Toast
Pasting now shows a toast like copying does, for regions, notes, devices, patterns and audio units pasted as pads or layers, from the shortcut and from the menus.

_Source: openDAW_

### More Chord Types
Chord detection knows 6th, add9, 9th, 11th and 13th chords, so Dm9 is no longer written as Dm7. The chord track's Shape menu groups its chord types into triads, suspended, 6th, 7th, 9th, 11th and 13th chords, with the new 6, m6, 6/9, add9, m(add9), 9, maj9, m9, m11, 13, maj13, m13, 7sus4 and 9sus4. Notes stacks 11th and 13th chords from the scale, voiced the way players do: without the fifth, and without an 11th that clashes with a major third. Extensions sound above the octave.

_Source: [#551](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/issues/551)_

### DAWproject Exchange
A DAWproject exported by openDAW imports back into openDAW with its tracks, regions, clips, automation, sends, groups, markers, tempo and signature changes, and every device with its settings. WebCLAP plugins export as CLAP plugins with their sound and automation, so Bitwig Studio and other DAWs load their own build of the same plugin. Importing works the other way round: a CLAP plugin that exists as a WebCLAP loads with its settings, scenes become clips, and anything openDAW cannot carry over is listed after the import.

_Source: [#563](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/issues/563) by Trinitou_

### Automatable Aux Sends
The gain and pan knobs of an aux send have the same context menu as the other mixer controls, so they can be automated, modulated and mapped to a MIDI controller. Their automation lanes are named after the target bus, for example Send Reverb, and follow when the bus is renamed or the send is rewired.

_Source: [#564](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/issues/564) by BLyndon_

### Load Large Plugins on Request
The device browser loads a WebCLAP, soundfont or stock preset up to 10 MB at once. A bigger one that is not stored on this device is only selected at first: its card shows the details and a Load button with its size appears in its row. The limit is set in MB next to the buttons and in the preferences, 0 loads everything at once. Enter, a double click or Ok load it.

_Source: openDAW_

### Split Devices Name Their Entries
Stereo Split shows L and R and Frequency Split its bands (Low, Low Mid, High Mid, High), in the entry list and in the switch buttons inside an entry. Changing the band count no longer moves the rows.

_Source: openDAW_

### Note Editor Fields
Every property field of the selected notes is draggable, up increases. Position and duration drag each part on its own (bar, quarter, semiquaver, ticks), Alt drags 4 times finer. A click without moving still edits the text, Escape cancels, and one drag is one undo step. Undo updates the fields too.

_Source: openDAW_

### Upload Saves the Project First
SoundCloud and Stage save the project before uploading, so the uploaded track and the saved project always match.

_Source: openDAW_

### Shared Projects Keep Your Samples
Opening a shared project adds the samples and soundfonts you do not have yet and never replaces the ones you already have. Projects from other websites ask before opening and say that they may contain scripts.

_Source: openDAW_

### Mixdowns Fade Out at the Length Limit
A mixdown that keeps sounding until its length limit, for example a project without regions, fades out over its last second instead of stopping hard.

_Source: openDAW_

### WebCLAPs Without a Window
A WebCLAP that shows everything in its device panel and has no window of its own no longer offers Open UI, which opened its panel a second time.

_Source: openDAW_

### Storage Scan Status
The storage item in the footer reads "scanning..." while openDAW measures the storage.

_Source: openDAW_

## Fixes

### Minus Sign in Number Fields
A minus sign could not be typed into number fields on keyboard layouts where it sits on another key than on a US keyboard, a German layout for example.

_Source: [#572](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/pull/572) by BLyndon_

### Soundfonts With an Empty Name Import
Some soundfonts, saved by Polyphone for example, were refused as unreadable because their name was empty. Soundfonts are now read by a more forgiving reader, such a file imports under its file name, and the Soundfont device and the Sampler also accept compressed SF3 and DLS files.

_Source: [#464](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/issues/464) by kauzofficial_

### Soundfont Device Plays Zone Tuning
The Soundfont device ignored the coarse tune, fine tune and pitch correction of a soundfont, so instruments tuned that way played out of tune. It applies them now. Projects using such a soundfont sound different, now in tune.

_Source: [#550](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/issues/550) by BeatMaxPrediction_

### Automation Reaches Self-Playing Instruments on Time
Instruments that play their own patterns, Cubed and Guitar, read an automated value only after they had planned the notes of a short audio block, so a pattern switched by automation exactly on a step still played the old pattern on that step. Automation now comes first and the instrument plays whatever is in charge at that moment.

_Source: openDAW_

### Pasting an Audio Unit With Notes
Paste AudioUnit as Layer with Drop copied notes no longer fails silently when the pasted unit's regions hold notes.

_Source: openDAW_

### Scripts With WebCLAP, Korpus and Modular Devices
Scripts no longer fail on projects that hold WebCLAP plugins, Korpus, Modular or placeholders of unknown devices. Such devices can be renamed, bypassed, moved and removed, and the instrument can be replaced, their parameters are not reachable yet.

_Source: [#553](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/issues/553)_

### Nextcloud Saved Logins
Saved logins offered by the browser in the Nextcloud connect dialog can be picked again.

_Source: openDAW_

### Recording Automation of Large Plugins
Recording automation on a track that holds a large plugin, Surge XT for example, crackled more and more until the engine stopped. Each step of the growing recording made the engine reconnect every parameter of the track. It now updates only the recorded curve, and the recording sends far fewer updates.

_Source: [#567](https://github.com/andremichelle/openDAW/issues/567) by Trinitou_

### Cloud Backup No Longer Stalls
The cloud backup could hang on "Collecting all sample domains..." when Chrome never answered a storage request under heavy load. It now gives up on such a request after 10 seconds and tries again three times before the backup fails with a Retry button. The backup dialog has a Cancel button.

### Full Browser Storage No Longer Fails a Project Load
A library sample or soundfont that downloaded fine but did not fit into the browser storage ended in an error and the project did not open. It now plays from memory and downloads again next time. A save that does not fit fails cleanly instead of leaving a cut file behind.

_Source: Riffle_

### Machines That Cannot Run the Audio Engine
When the audio engine cannot start at all on a machine, openDAW now says so in a dialog instead of offering a Restart Engine button that fails again every time.

_Source: openDAW_

### Tubular Cutoff at Low Sample Rates
Tubular stopped the audio engine when its cutoff was turned up high while the browser ran at a sample rate below 44.1 kHz, as Firefox can. The filter now stays stable at every sample rate.

_Source: openDAW_

### Closing a Popout Window With a Tooltip
Closing a popout window while a tooltip was shown in it could crash openDAW in Firefox. The tooltip now closes with the window.

_Source: openDAW_

### Sample Rates Below 44.1 kHz
openDAW does not start at a sample rate below 44.1 kHz, which Firefox can run at on some devices. Instead of failing in odd ways, a dialog says that this sample rate is not supported.

_Source: openDAW_
